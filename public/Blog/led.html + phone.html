SASO & SABER Certification Guide for GCC Importers (2026)

Last updated: November 2026 · 6 min read

If you're importing products from China to Saudi Arabia, SASO and SABER compliance is non-negotiable. Without it, your goods will be held at the port, incurring daily demurrage fees that can exceed the value of the shipment.

This guide explains the process, costs, and timelines — so you can plan your imports without surprises.

1. What is SASO and SABER?

SASO (Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization) is the Saudi standards body. SABER is the mandatory electronic conformity platform where Saudi-registered importers register products and obtain clearance certificates linked to Fasah customs.

Every commercial shipment entering Saudi Arabia requires two layers of approval:

  • Product Certificate of Conformity (PCoC): Registers the product model against applicable Saudi Technical Regulations and remains valid for 1 year.
  • Shipment Certificate of Conformity (SCoC): Issued per individual consignment before vessel arrival, required for both regulated and self-declared products.

2. Which Products Need SABER?

Regulated categories include:

  • LED lighting and electrical appliances
  • Phone accessories, chargers, and wireless electronics (which may also require IECEE or CST approval)
  • Cleaning chemicals and detergents
  • Building materials, toys, and automotive parts

Verify your exact 12-digit HS code and technical regulation on the official SABER portal before production begins.

3. The SABER Process (Step by Step)

  1. Register your company on SABER as a Saudi importer of record (requires a valid Saudi Commercial Registration / CR number)
  2. Request PCoC (Product Certificate) — enter the HS code, product barcode, specifications, and factory details
  3. Submit accredited test reports — ISO/IEC 17025 lab reports (such as IEC/CB reports) valid within 3 years, plus bilingual product labels and photos
  4. Receive PCoC — typically 3–7 working days after Conformity Assessment Body (CAB) review
  5. Request SCoC (Shipment Certificate) for each consignment using the commercial invoice, packing list, and active PCoC — issued within 1–2 days
  6. Present SCoC via Fasah for smooth Saudi customs clearance

5. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • HS code mismatch: Ensure the HS code on your SABER PCoC/SCoC matches your commercial invoice and customs declaration exactly
  • Waiting until goods reach port: Post-arrival SCoC applications trigger violations and demurrage; Letters of Undertaking are no longer accepted
  • Missing product barcodes or labels: Every registered SKU requires a valid barcode and clear origin/rating markings on the unit
  • Assuming all factories have SASO-ready reports: Verify valid ISO/IEC 17025 test reports before paying a production deposit
