SASO & SABER Certification Guide for GCC Importers (2026)

Last updated: November 2026 · 6 min read

If you're importing products from China to Saudi Arabia, SASO and SABER compliance is non-negotiable. Without it, your goods will be held at the port, incurring daily demurrage fees that can exceed the value of the shipment.

This guide explains the process, costs, and timelines — so you can plan your imports without surprises.

What is SASO and SABER?

SASO (Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization) is the Saudi standards body. SABER is the online platform where importers register products and obtain certificates.

Since 2019, all regulated products entering Saudi Arabia must have a Product Certificate of Conformity (PCoC) and a Shipment Certificate of Conformity (SCoC) issued through SABER.

Which Products Need SABER?

Regulated categories include:

  • LED lighting and electrical appliances
  • Phone accessories and electronics
  • Cleaning chemicals and detergents
  • Building materials, toys, automotive parts

Check the latest SASO technical regulations on the SABER portal.

The SABER Process (Step by Step)

  1. Register your company on SABER as an importer (requires CR number)
  2. Request PCoC (Product Certificate) — valid for 1 year, covers product specs and test reports
  3. Submit test reports from an accredited lab (most Chinese factories provide IEC/CB reports; some need retesting to SASO standards)
  4. Receive PCoC — typically 3–7 working days after submission
  5. Request SCoC (Shipment Certificate) for each shipment — issued within 1–2 days
  6. Present SCoC to customs for clearance

Common Pitfalls

  • Using test reports that don't match SASO standards — always confirm with your certification body first
  • Applying for SCoC before PCoC is approved — SCoC requires a valid PCoC
  • Shipping before SCoC is issued — customs will not release goods without it
  • Assuming all Chinese factories have SASO-ready test reports — verify before ordering
